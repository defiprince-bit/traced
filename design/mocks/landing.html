The story

Every circuit leaves a trace.

TapeOut circuits are built from NAND and LATCH gates. They can reuse each other by REF, and that credit is written into the bytes. So is a copy.

01

A circuit is taped out.Its netlist bytes and owner go on-chain.

02

Someone reuses it by REF.The credit is part of their bytes.

03

Someone copies it silently.Same bytes, no REF back.

Four shapes. One glance.

1–3 NAND/LATCH gates · no REF

A trivial gate like NOT or AND. Easy to make, so it’s often copied many times.

Honesty

Honest about what a chain can’t show.

01

Free runs leave no trace

eval() is a view call. It costs nothing and writes nothing on-chain.

02

So we don’t count usage

Nobody can see who runs a circuit, including us. We won’t guess a number.

03

Read-only, always

No wallet, no signing. Every lookup is a public read on X Layer.

04

Earliest isn’t proof

The likely original is picked by tape-out block, or by ID on one processor. It’s a strong hint, not a verdict.

05

Badges are our labels

Toy, Wrapper, Library and Miner-sized are Traced heuristics, not TapeOut classes.

06

Same bytes, nothing more

A copy check compares netlist bytes. It can’t see intent, and REF reuse is never a copy.
